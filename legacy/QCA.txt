Quantum Cryptography is a field of cryptography that uses the principles of quantum mechanics to develop cryptographic algorithms that are resistant to attacks from eavesdroppers.

1. Key Generation phase:
a. Alice and Bob agree on the quantum channel and the classical channel to be used for communication.
b. Alice generates a random string of qubits and sends them to Bob through the quantum channel.
c. Bob selects a random basis for measuring the qubits and measures each qubit in the chosen basis.

2. Error detection phase:
a. Bob publicly announces the basis he used to measure each qubit over the classical channel.
b. Alice compares the basis used by Bob to her own basis for each qubit to detect any errors that may have occurred during transmission.
c. Alice discards any qubits that were measured in the wrong basis and announces the positions of these qubits over the classical channel.

3. Error correction phase:
a. Alice and Bob exchange information over the classical channel to correct any errors that were detected.
b. Alice sends Bob the basis she used to encode each qubit that was measured in the wrong basis.
c. Bob measures each of these qubits again using the correct basis and announces the results to Alice over the classical channel.

4. Privacy amplification phase:
a. Alice and Bob use a hash function to extract a shared secret key from the corrected qubit values.
b. Alice and Bob perform a protocol to generate a shared random seed to be used as an input to the hash function.
c. Alice and Bob use the shared secret key to encrypt and decrypt their messages.

Here are some security measures incorporated in the algorithm:

1. Authentication: The algorithm ensures that only Alice and Bob can communicate with each other by authenticating each other's identities.

2. Secrecy: The algorithm ensures that the key used for encryption and decryption is kept secret from any eavesdroppers. The use of the quantum channel ensures that any attempt to 
intercept or measure the qubits will disturb their state, making it impossible for an eavesdropper to obtain the key without being detected.

3. Error detection and correction: The algorithm includes an error detection and correction mechanism to detect and correct any errors that may occur during transmission. This 
mechanism ensures that the final key obtained by Alice and Bob is error-free and secure.

4. Privacy amplification: The algorithm includes a privacy amplification phase to extract a shorter, but secure, shared key from the raw key generated during the algorithm. This 
ensures that even if an eavesdropper has obtained some information about the raw key, they cannot use it to derive the final shared key used for encryption and decryption.
