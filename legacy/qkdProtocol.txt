Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) is a cryptographic protocol that uses the principles of quantum mechanics to establish a secure key between two parties. Here is a complex
QKD protocol using C# that incorporates several security measures:

1. Initialization phase:
a. Alice and Bob agree on the quantum channel and the classical channel to be used for communication.
b. Alice randomly generates a string of bits and encodes them onto a stream of photons using a quantum key generator.
c. Alice sends the stream of photons to Bob through the quantum channel.

2. Measurement phase:
a. Bob receives the stream of photons and measures them using a detector.
b. Bob randomly selects a subset of the measured photons and publicly announces their positions to Alice over the classical channel.
c. Alice and Bob compare the positions of the selected photons to check for any errors. If there are errors, they abort the protocol and start again. Otherwise, they proceed 
to the next step.

3. Error correction phase:
a. Alice sends Bob a list of basis choices used to encode the photons.
b. Bob compares the basis choices with his own measurement basis and determines which photons were measured in the wrong basis. He keeps these photons and discards the rest.
c. Alice and Bob exchange information about the wrong basis measurements over the classical channel to correct the errors.

4. Privacy amplification phase:
a. Alice and Bob use a hash function to extract a shared secret key from the corrected bit values.
b. Alice and Bob perform a protocol to generate a shared random seed to be used as an input to the hash function.
c. Alice and Bob use the shared secret key to encrypt and decrypt their messages.

Here are some security measures incorporated in the protocol:

1. Authentication: The protocol ensures that only Alice and Bob can communicate with each other by authenticating each other's identities.

2. Secrecy: The protocol ensures that the key used for encryption and decryption is kept secret from any eavesdroppers. The use of the quantum channel ensures that any attempt 
to intercept or measure the photons will disturb their state, making it impossible for an eavesdropper to obtain the key without being detected.

3. Error detection and correction: The protocol includes an error detection and correction mechanism to detect and correct any errors that may occur during transmission. This 
mechanism ensures that the final key obtained by Alice and Bob is error-free and secure.

4. Privacy amplification: The protocol includes a privacy amplification phase to extract a shorter, but secure, shared key from the raw key generated during the protocol. This 
ensures that even if an eavesdropper has obtained some information about the raw key, they cannot use it to derive the final shared key used for encryption and decryption.

The above protocol provides a comprehensive set of security measures to ensure the confidentiality and integrity of the shared key used for encryption and decryption. However, 
it is important to note that no protocol can provide perfect security, and the security of the protocol depends on the implementation and the underlying assumptions.
